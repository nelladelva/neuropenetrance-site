Think of it like a weather forecast — for mental health risk

A forecast never promises rain. It reads the signals in the atmosphere and gives you a probability you can plan around — take the umbrella, move the picnic. NeuroPenetrance applies exactly that logic to human biology.

Instead of a yes-or-no genetic answer, we produce a measured, interpretable estimate of biological risk — built from what a person's own cells are actually doing, not from population statistics about people who happen to share their mutation.

The core insight

DNA is the recipe. It doesn't tell you how the meal turned out. We look at the meal — the living neurons that recipe produced — and read the difference.

The three steps, in plain language

INPUT

Real human neurons

A skin or blood sample is reprogrammed into stem cells and grown into brain cells carrying that person's exact genetics.

MODEL

Pattern detection

Why cells, and not just a DNA sequence

There is a whole category of company offering DNA-based risk scores — polygenic scores for prospective parents, or for cancer risk. They all work from the same raw material: the static sequence you were born with.

Our approach is different in kind, not just in degree. We measure the functional consequence of a variant inside living neurons. That reveals how other genes, the cellular environment, and a person's whole genetic background combine to either amplify a mutation's effect or buffer it away. A DNA sequence simply cannot answer that question — it is the wrong instrument.

This matters more, not less, as the genetics field matures. A 2026 re-analysis in the European Journal of Human Genetics found that widely used DNA-derived penetrance figures had been substantially overstated, with several variants previously flagged as risky recalculating to near-zero penetrance for intellectual disability. Statistics about populations are a poor substitute for measurement of an individual.

The honest test we set ourselves

The prediction engine was deliberately validated on a hard case first: whole-blood gene expression data from real patients. Blood is a noisier, more indirect signal than purpose-grown neurons — if the pipeline could find real structure there, it would find more in richer data.

It did. The pipeline pulled out a validated, interpretable signal under those harder conditions, at accuracy comparable to published academic benchmarks. That is a meaningful indicator of how it should perform on the cleaner, purpose-generated neuronal data our lab platform produces — data tied to known mutations and controlled experiments rather than a one-off blood draw. The full numbers are on the Evidence page.

Who benefits

Three groups, three different wins

The same underlying measurement answers a different question for each of them.

Clinicians

Something concrete to tell the family

Psychiatrists and genetic counsellors currently have to explain that a result is "uncertain". A functional risk profile gives them an evidence-based signal to support counselling, monitoring decisions and earlier referral — rather than another probability drawn from a population table.

Biotech & pharma

Trials that aren't sabotaged by hidden variability

Companies developing neuro-therapeutics can select participants by what their cells actually do, and test candidate compounds on human neurons carrying real patient mutations — before committing to a trial with a 90%-plus failure rate.

Researchers

A dataset that doesn't exist yet

Every profiled line adds molecular data tied to a known mutation and a controlled perturbation. That supports causal questions, not just correlational ones — and the analysis pipeline is open-source.
