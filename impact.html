Built for everyone who has to act on a genetic result

Families, clinicians, drug developers and researchers are all stuck on the same missing piece of information. One measurement answers a different question for each of them.

Families & patients

An answer instead of an open question

Right now a family can receive a genetic finding and be told, truthfully, that nobody knows what it means for their child. A functional risk signal changes that conversation — it supports earlier monitoring where risk looks high, and genuine reassurance where the biology looks resilient. Earlier support is the single biggest lever in conditions where diagnosis currently takes ten years.

Clinicians

Psychiatrists, genetic counsellors, paediatric neurologists

Genetic counsellors are the people who have to explain uncertainty to families, and psychiatrists are the ones asked what happens next. Both currently work from population statistics that a 2026 re-analysis showed can be substantially wrong. A measurement taken from the patient's own cells gives them something specific to that person — evidence to guide counselling, follow-up intervals and referral decisions.

Biotech & pharma

Companies testing neuro-therapeutic agents

More than 90% of clinical trials in brain disorders fail, and hidden biological variability between patients is a major contributor. Being able to sort trial candidates by what their neurons actually do — and to test a candidate compound on human neurons carrying real patient mutations before the trial starts — attacks that failure rate at its source.

Scientists

The research community

Every profiled cell line adds molecular data linked to a known mutation and a controlled experimental perturbation — the kind of data that supports questions about cause, not just correlation. The analysis pipeline is open-source, the failed hypotheses are published alongside the successful ones, and collaboration proposals are welcome.

What changes at the system level

Mental ill health costs EU economies roughly 1.7% of GDP a year and absorbs about 6% of total health spending. Almost all of that is spent downstream — after a crisis, after a diagnosis, after years of missed support. A reliable early risk signal moves spending upstream, where it is both cheaper and more humane.

  • Earlier support instead of late crisis care. Intervening during the developmental window, rather than after a decade of symptoms, is where the health and economic returns actually are.
  • Fewer failed trials. Every avoided late-stage failure in a brain-disorder programme frees hundreds of millions for treatments that can work.
  • Genomic medicine that finally delivers. Sequencing has become cheap and routine; interpretation has not kept pace. Functional measurement is the missing half.
  • European data sovereignty. Built in Berlin, under EU data and medical-device rules, with EU patients and EU research partners.

Where this sits in European healthcare

A test like this would eventually be regulated in the EU under the In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation (IVDR 2017/746), most likely in the higher-risk Class C category given its clinical use. That is a serious, multi-year pathway — and it is the right one. In the meantime, the same technology is immediately usable as a research and pharma service, which is where the business starts.

Being clear about the stage

NeuroPenetrance is a research-stage company. The laboratory service and research platform come first; a regulated clinical diagnostic is a longer-term goal, not a current product. Nothing on this site should be read as a medical device claim or as medical advice.
