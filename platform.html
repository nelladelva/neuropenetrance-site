Two engines that make each other stronger

One is a laboratory service that measures what a mutation does inside real human neurons. The other is software that learns from every sample the lab produces. Each one makes the other more valuable.

The business in one sentence

We sell a lab service today, and the data that service generates becomes an AI product that nobody else can build — because nobody else has the data.

ENGINE 1 · REVENUE FROM DAY ONE

The laboratory service

We take patient stem cell lines and grow them into the specific types of brain cell that matter for these conditions — inhibitory neurons, cortical neurons, and small 3D "mini-brain" organoids. Then we measure them deeply: which genes are switched on, which proteins are present, how the DNA is packaged, and how the neurons actually fire.

To see how a mutation behaves under pressure, we also dial the underlying biology up and down — using gene-edited cell lines and drug-based tuning of the SHH pathway, the developmental system that Dr. Hammes-Lewin's laboratory has studied for years. Patient stem cell lines carrying human SHH mutations are available from day one through our collaborator Dr. Valérie Dupé at the University of Rennes.

  • Penetrance Risk ProfileA quantitative molecular signature separating genetic vulnerability from resilience.
  • Therapeutic Sensitivity ReportHow those cells respond to drug-based modulation — the patient's window for intervention.
  • Biomarker PanelA ranked set of molecular targets for clinical follow-up or trial enrolment.
ENGINE 2 · THE COMPOUNDING ASSET

The AI biomarker platform

Every sample Engine 1 runs produces thousands of molecular measurements. Engine 2 is the machine learning layer trained on that growing library — classifiers that learn which biological patterns predict illness and which predict resilience, and rank the strongest candidate biomarkers by pathway, cell type and developmental stage.

Crucially, this training data is not a public dataset anyone can download. It is tied to known mutations and controlled experiments, which means the models can pursue cause rather than mere correlation. Delivered as software to pharma R&D and diagnostic lab partners, it scales without a matching increase in lab cost.

  • Already built and validatedThe pipeline exists and has been tested end-to-end on public patient data — see the Evidence page.
  • No technical co-founder requiredThe founder's own data science background (Python, SQL, BigQuery, scikit-learn) covers this engine in year one.
  • Interpretable by designOutputs are ranked and explainable, which is what clinical and regulatory use demands.

The flywheel

Each service contract generates proprietary training data that sharpens the AI. A sharper AI makes each new service contract more valuable. A generic stem cell contract lab has the wet-lab capability but not the AI layer built for this specific question; an academic core facility has neither the commercial throughput nor the incentive. That compounding loop is the defensible part of the business.

Where we sit in the market

No company we have identified combines patient-derived neuronal profiling with a penetrance or disease-risk product built specifically for neurodevelopmental conditions. The surrounding neighbourhood — precision psychiatry biomarkers, stem-cell-based drug-response prediction, polygenic risk scoring — does contain funded, credentialed companies, and several could plausibly extend toward this niche.

NeuroKaire is the closest technical analogue — a similar combination of patient-derived neurons and machine learning — but it answers a different clinical question. It helps choose a drug for someone already diagnosed with depression. We are asking whether a person carrying a risk variant will develop a disorder in the first place. Different question, different data, different customer moment.

How we make money

Engine 1 is fee-for-service: a Penetrance Risk Profile at €8,000–15,000 per sample set, a full Therapeutic Sensitivity Report at €20,000–35,000, and custom multi-patient cohort contracts for pharma clients. Engine 2 adds a software licence from year two, at €2,000–8,000 per month for pharma biomarker teams — revenue that grows without a matching increase in laboratory cost.
