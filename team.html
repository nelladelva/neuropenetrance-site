Two people, both sides of the problem

NeuroPenetrance needs someone who can work at the lab bench and someone who can build the models. It happens to be the same person — supported by the scientist whose laboratory defined the underlying biology.

ND

Nella Christie Delva, PhD

Founder — Neuroscientist & Data Scientist

Nella's doctoral research at Florida State University examined dopamine signalling in the prefrontal cortex and its role in stress resilience and mood — in other words, why some brains cope with pressure and others don't. That question is the direct ancestor of this company.

A Fulbright Research Fellowship brought her to the Max Delbrück Center for Molecular Medicine in Berlin, where she worked hands-on in the Hammes-Lewin laboratory with patient-derived stem cells: growing them, turning them into neurons, and imaging what those neurons became. That is the scientific foundation of the laboratory engine.

She then built and validated the machine learning engine independently, pairing that bench background with formal data science training in Python, SQL and machine learning. She is also a McKnight Doctoral Fellow and a recipient of the Germanistic Society of America Award.

AH

Dr. Annette Hammes-Lewin

Academic Mentor — Max Delbrück Center, Berlin

Group Leader for Molecular Pathways in Cortical Development at the Max Delbrück Center for Molecular Medicine. Her laboratory studies Sonic Hedgehog signalling in forebrain development — the developmental system that sits underneath NeuroPenetrance's entire model of incomplete penetrance.

Her group provides the validated stem cell lines, the differentiation protocols and the research infrastructure behind the laboratory engine, committed as in-kind support to the company.

Why a team of two is the right size right now

Most companies in this space need at least two founders: a wet-lab scientist and a machine learning engineer. Here, both skill sets sit in one person — experimental neuroscience on one side, data analytics and machine learning on the other.

That means both engines can be developed in year one without hiring an external technical co-founder, which is a real advantage of cost and speed in the earliest and riskiest phase of a company. The mentorship and laboratory infrastructure from the Hammes-Lewin group cover the rest.

NeuroPenetrance is actively looking for collaborators, pilot partners and advisors — particularly clinical geneticists, biotech R&D teams working on neuro-therapeutics, and research groups with relevant patient cell lines. If that sounds like you, get in touch.
