The same gene. Two very different lives.

A genetic test can tell you that you carry a risk gene. It cannot tell you whether you will ever become ill. NeuroPenetrance closes that gap — by growing a person's own brain cells in the lab and measuring what their biology actually does.

The gap we close

Genetics tells you the risk. It doesn't tell you the outcome.

Doctors call this incomplete penetrance: two people can carry exactly the same mutation and end up with completely different lives. One develops autism, schizophrenia or severe depression. The other never has a symptom. Right now, no test can tell a family which one they are looking at.

Today

A result nobody can act on

Families receive a genetic finding with no idea what it means for their child's future. Clinicians are left explaining uncertainty rather than giving guidance.

Today

Trials that fail for the wrong reason

Drug trials in brain disorders fail more than 90% of the time — partly because patients who look identical on paper have very different underlying biology.

Our answer

Measure the biology, not just the letters

We grow neurons from a patient's own cells and read what the mutation actually does inside them — then turn that into a clear, interpretable risk signal.

How it works

Think of it like a weather forecast — for brain health

A forecast doesn't promise rain. It reads the signals in the atmosphere and gives you a probability you can plan around. We do the same thing with biology.

STEP 1

Grow the cells

A small skin or blood sample is reprogrammed into stem cells, then grown into real human neurons carrying that person's exact genetics.

→
STEP 2

Read the biology

We measure thousands of molecular signals in those neurons — which genes switch on, which proteins appear, how the cells behave and fire.

→
STEP 3

Get a clear signal

Machine learning turns those measurements into a readable risk profile — showing which biological patterns point toward illness, and which point toward resilience.
